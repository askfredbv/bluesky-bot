# @askfred.be in 2027: one-page plan

*This plan is valid until we make a better one. At every quarterly check we ask whether it still is. A better plan replaces this one and gets a new date, and the old one goes to the archive.*

*Approved by Frederik, 16 Nov 2026. Built on Frederik's answers, the last 90 posts and their reactions (public Bluesky API), the two OpenAI reviews and a feasibility check of the repo.*

**For whom.** IT enthusiasts and professionals, curious people who get things done, and leaders in the IT sector.
**The reaction we want.** "I didn't know that."
**Time budget.** Half an hour a day from Frederik, replies included: about 3.5 hours a week.

## Where it stands (16 Nov 2026)

- 105 followers. Over the last 90 top-level posts, the 45 morning news posts got 30 likes and the 45 afternoon posts got 15.
- The afternoon draws from two fixed lists of evergreen topics (21 Mentor, 24 Strategist): documentation, dated plans, sell-in versus sell-through. These are the dull subjects. "In five years…" opened 7 posts in a month, and the AI-ethics log post ran twice, 16 days apart.
- The morning is narrower than the audience: 13 of 45 posts were about a benchmark, and all ~27 feeds cover AI.
- The posts that most often get "I didn't know that" already exist: the Pioneer posts (Mel, PGP printed as a book, Bellard, RFC 1149). Today they are a 35% side-branch.
- Real people do reply (Rhea, Prachit Parikh, Aries Spring). Many of the other repliers are accounts that look like bots themselves.

## Guardrails (six, and only six)

1. **True to the source.** A post claims only what its source says, with no invented numbers, versions or quotes.
2. **Positive and useful.** No negativity, no clickbait, no politics. A strong, specific point is welcome: surprise comes from the fact, not from the tone.
3. **Skip rather than embarrass.** When a post fails the source check or the voice check, skip the run. Taste is judged at the monthly read, not by blocking runs.
4. **The replies are Frederik's.** The bot never answers people on its own.
5. **One account, one voice.** Bluesky and Mastodon get the same post. Only the mechanics differ.
6. **Live is the proof.** A change is done when the public feed shows it working, not when CI is green.

Every other rule in the repo has to earn its place against these six, or it goes to the archive.

## Guidelines for how work gets done

- **Start from the feed, not from the code.** Every change names the posts it is meant to fix.
- **One change, one measure, one date.** A content change is merged with its check date in the issue. Two content changes don't overlap.
- **Allow, then check.** The default answer to a new direction is "yes, with a check date", not "no, unless". A "never" needs an incident behind it and gets reviewed once a year.
- **A short repo memory.** `BACKLOG.md` holds the goal and the open queue and fits on one screen. History goes to the archive. Lessons go into tests, not into warnings.
- **Infrastructure follows a trigger.** From the OpenAI reviews, take explicit mode (#265), a run-outcome record with a phone alert, pause and dry-run, and a list of dependencies with a Plan B for each, plus one rehearsed Gist restore. All by 31 Mar 2027. A full cloud migration (scheduler, container job, transactional store) stays off the table until a trigger fires: Gist state lost or corrupted, or more than 2 missed runs a month for two months running. Neither review touches what Frederik is actually unhappy with, which is the subjects.

## First three moves

1. **The afternoon is about what is happening.** The afternoon post no longer starts from a list topic. It starts from the day's material: news the morning didn't use, how-things-work facts, and computing history (Pioneer becomes a main source, no longer a 35% side-branch). Mentor or Strategist decides the *angle*, not the subject. The fixed lists stay only as a fallback, with a 30-day per-mode cooldown and the bot's last 30 days of posts in context (issue #270, widened). Build it now and merge after the #230 readout on 24 Nov. Check on 22 Dec: zero repeats, and Frederik's 20-post read.
2. **Widen the beat to match the audience.** Add feeds for infrastructure, security, open source, hardware and IT-industry moves, including European ones, and give "surprising" a place in scoring. "Surprising" means a concrete fact an IT professional likely doesn't know yet: history, how something works, a first of its kind. The prompt asks for it, and scoring favours it over another leaderboard number. Cap benchmark stories at one a week. Start in January 2027, after move 1 is checked. Check on 1 Feb: at most 4 benchmark posts in the 4 weeks, and Frederik's 20-post read.
3. **Rewrite the rulebook to serve this plan.** `BACKLOG.md` becomes one page: the goal, this plan's objectives and the open issues. `AGENTS.md` replaces its long list of "do not" lines with the six guardrails above, and drops "prefer changes with no recurring human cost", which no longer fits half an hour a day.
